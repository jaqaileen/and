BODIES OF WATER (1/2)

ABOUT

Bodies of Water explores the concept of hydrofeminism which proposes that the human body is not a closed or isolated entity, but a porous body of water in constant exchange with the world around it. Rather than existing separately from nature, our bodies circulate through larger biological and ecological systems. Through this lens, women’s bodies can be understood not as contained forms, but as aqueous and relational—continuously interacting with, shaped by, and ultimately returning to water.

THE PROCESS

This installation translates these ideas into a spatial and visual experience through eighteen cyanotype prints on fabric. The pieces are arranged in three layers allowing the translucent material to overlap and create depth, movement, and shifting visual relationships as viewers move through the space. Each image pairs a part of the human body with elements of water, shells, or aquatic life, carefully selected so that their forms echo or mirror one another—hair resembling river lines, skin mirroring water ripples, or bodily contours reflecting aquatic animals. These visual connections blur the boundary between body and environment, suggesting a shared material language rooted in water.
